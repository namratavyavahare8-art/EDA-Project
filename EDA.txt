Customer Purchase EDA



🎯Objective

This EDA explores customer demographics (age, gender, state) and purchase Amount to show who the customers are, where they are, and how much they spend. The findings can guide targeting and marketing decisions.


📈Analysis Performed

Four plots cover the relationship, distribution and regional views.

          Plot                                         Purpose

  Scatter (Age vs Amount)         :   Check for a relationship between age and spend

  Histogram + KDE (Age)           :   Understand the age distribution

  Box plot (Amount)               :   Show spread, median and outliers in spending

  Heatmap (State × Gender)        :   Compare customer counts by region and gender


🔑Key Findings

Customers are mostly women in their late 20s to mid 30s, concentrated in a few large states, and age does not predict how much they spend.

1. Age is concentrated in the late 20s to mid 30s. The distribution peaks at roughly 28 to 32 and is right-skewed. Counts drop sharply after about 56, and customers over 60 are sparse (under about 50 per bin).

2. Age has no visible effect on spending. The scatter plot shows the same horizontal bands at every age, so age alone does not predict Amount.

3. Amount clusters at discrete levels. Bands near ₹2k, 5k, 8k, 12k, 16k and 19–23k suggest fixed price points or product tiers, not a continuous spread.

4. Spending is moderately right-skewed. The median is about ₹8,100 and the IQR runs from roughly ₹5,400 to ₹12,700. A few high-end outliers sit above about ₹23,500, and there are no low outliers.

5. Women are the dominant group. They outnumber men by roughly 2 to 3 times in every state.

6. Customers are geographically concentrated. Uttar Pradesh (3,402 F / 1,411 M), Maharashtra (2,814 / 997), Karnataka (2,266 / 975) and Delhi (1,872 / 872) lead. Telangana, Punjab and Rajasthan have the fewest.

Suggested Business Takeaways

Marketing effort is best aimed at the largest, clearest segment first.

• Focus on women aged about 20 to 40 in Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka and Delhi.

• Treat the 55+ segment as under-served, or check whether the drop reflects data collection limits.

• Investigate which products or categories drive the Amount bands.


✅Limitations and Next Steps

The four plots describe the data but do not yet explain the spending bands or the drop after age 56.

Check why the data thins out sharply after about 56 (binning, sampling, or real behavior).

Compare Amount distributions by gender and state, not just counts.

Add age groups (12–17, 18–25, 26–35, 36–55, 55+).

Analyze product category, occupation or marital status if available.

Run a correlation or hypothesis test (for example ANOVA) on Amount across gender and state.
